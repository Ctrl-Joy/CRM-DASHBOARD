Chapter 1

Getting started

Create your workspace, sign in and invite your team.

Start the app (local install)

  1. Install Node.js 20.11 or newer.
  2. In the project folder run npm run setup. It installs packages, creates server/.env with a random secret and creates the database.
  3. Run npm run dev and open http://localhost:5173.

For production, run npm run build then npm start; the API and the app are served together on one port (default 4000).

Create your account

  1. On the home page click Book a demo, or click Sign in and then Create an account.
  2. Enter your name, company, work email and a password of at least 8 characters.
  3. You land on the dashboard as the Admin of a brand-new organization.
Your workspace starts emptyNew accounts contain no sample data: no contacts, deals, leads or statistics. The only thing created for you is one empty pipeline called Sales Pipeline with five stages, because deals need a pipeline to live in. The dashboard shows a five-step “Welcome to your new CRM” checklist until you add your first records.

Invite your team

  1. Go to Settings → Users & quotas and click Add user.
  2. Enter their name, email, role (Sales Rep, Manager or Admin), a temporary password, an optional monthly quota and territory.
  3. Share the temporary password securely. They can change it in Settings → Profile.
  4. Optionally create teams in Settings → Teams and set a manager; managers then see their team’s records and dashboards.

Sign out and sessions

Use the avatar menu (top right) → Sign out. Changing your password signs you out on every other device. When an admin changes your role or deactivates you, your sessions end immediately.

Optional demo data

Running npm run seed creates a separate organization, Northwind Demo, with realistic sample data and five demo logins (password demo-password-2026). It never touches your own organization. Use it to explore before entering real data.

Chapter 3

The dashboard

Your home screen answers two questions: how are we doing, and what should I do next?

Views and filters

  • My dashboard: only records you own. Team dashboard: your team’s (managers) or the whole organization’s (admins). Admins and managers open on the Team view; reps see My dashboard only.
  • Date range: This month, Last 30 days, This quarter, This year. Every metric is compared with the previous period of the same length.
  • Pipeline (when you have more than one), and on the Team view: Owner, Team and Territory.
  • The dashboard refreshes automatically every minute.

Key metrics

Four headline cards show at the top; Show all 10 metrics reveals the rest. Each shows a green or red arrow with the % change against the previous period (for velocity and response time, lower is better).

Chapter 4

Working with lists

Leads, contacts, companies, deals (list view), tickets and products share one powerful table.

Search & filter

Type in Search… to match names, emails, phones and companies. Filters narrows by status, owner (Me / Unassigned / a person), source, dates and more. Clear resets everything.

Sort & page

Click a column header to sort; click again to reverse. Use the arrows at the bottom to page through 25 records at a time.

Saved views

Views → name the current search + filters → Save view. Tick Share with my team to make it shared. Pick a view to re-apply it later.

Inline edit

Some columns (lifecycle stage, lead status, ticket priority/status, deal stage) are drop-downs: change them right in the table. The change saves instantly and rolls back if it fails.

Bulk actions

Tick rows (or the header box for the page). A bar appears with Assign, Tag, a status/stage setter and Delete, depending on your permissions.

Import & export

⋯ → Export CSV downloads exactly what you’re filtering. Import CSV previews the file and imports up to 5,000 rows, then lists any rows it skipped and why.

Chapter 5

Leads

Capture interest, score it, route it to the right person and convert it into business.

Add leads

  • + New lead on the Leads page (or + New → Lead): name, email, phone, company, title, company size, source, status, owner, territory and campaign.
  • Import CSV from the list’s ⋯ menu.
  • Website / API capture: leads posted to your public form or API arrive automatically (see below).

Lead scoring (0–100)

Every lead gets a score that updates when it changes or when you log activity. The lead page explains exactly where the points came from.

SignalPoints
SourceReferral 25 · Partner 20 · Webinar 18 · Event 15 · Inbound API 15 · Website 12 · Paid ads 10 · Social 8 · Cold outreach 5 · Other 5
Job titleDecision-maker (CEO, CTO, founder, owner, VP…) 25 · Director / Head 18 · Manager / Lead 10 · other title 3
Company size1000+: 15 · 501–1000: 13 · 201–500: 12 · 51–200: 10 · 11–50: 6 · 1–10: 3
Contact dataBusiness email 6 (free webmail 3) · Phone 4 · Company given 3 · Left a message 5

Scores of 70+ show a 🔥 flame, 40–69 amber, below 40 grey. The AI insight on the lead page suggests the next move (for example “High-fit lead with no response yet. Call within the hour”).

Assignment

  • Leads you create belong to you unless you choose another owner (needs the reassign permission).
  • Inbound leads from the form/API are auto-assigned using round-robin across active sales reps, or by territory (falling back to round-robin). Set this in Settings → Organization. The new owner gets a notification.
  • The list shows First response; leads still waiting are highlighted in amber. Logging the first call/email/meeting records the response time and moves a New lead to Contacted.

Convert a lead

  1. Open the lead and click Convert.
  2. Keep Also create a deal ticked to create a deal; set its title, value, expected close date, pipeline and stage.
  3. Click Convert. Nexora creates the contact, links or creates the company named on the lead, creates the deal, and moves the lead’s activities, tasks and files across. The lead is marked Qualified and shows View contact.

Public capture form and API Admin

Settings → Lead capture & API shows your hosted form link (/f/<key>), the POST endpoint and a sample request. Required fields: first name and email. Website forms can post directly. Rotate key invalidates the old link immediately.

Chapter 6

Contacts

The people you sell to and support, with their whole history in one place.

The contacts list

  • Columns: name and title, company, email, lifecycle stage (editable inline), owner, tags, last contacted and created date.
  • Filter by lifecycle stage, status, source, owner and created date; filter by tag; save views.
  • Lifecycle stages: Subscriber → Lead → MQL → SQL → Opportunity → Customer → Evangelist. Winning a deal automatically moves its contact to Customer.

The 360° contact page

  • AI summary: when you last spoke, how many touchpoints in 30 days, open deals and their stage, open tickets, and the next planned step (or a nudge to plan one).
  • About: email (click to email), phone (click to call), owner, source, last contacted, custom fields and tags.
  • Company card, linking to the company page.
  • Tabs: Timeline (filter by calls, emails, meetings, notes), Deals (with risk flags and New deal), Tasks, Emails and Tickets (New ticket).
  • Files: attach proposals, contracts and other documents.

Find and merge duplicates

  1. On Contacts click Find duplicates. Nexora groups contacts with the same email or the same full name.
  2. The oldest record in each group is kept. Click Merge next to a duplicate.
  3. Its deals, activities, tasks, tickets, files and tags move to the kept contact; empty fields are filled from the duplicate; the duplicate is removed. The merge is recorded in the audit log.

Chapter 8

Deals and the pipeline

Track every opportunity from first conversation to signed contract.

Create a deal

+ New → Deal, + New deal on the Deals page, + Add deal at the bottom of a board column, from a contact or company, or by converting a lead. Fields: title, value, currency, pipeline and stage, contact, company, owner, expected close date, win probability (leave blank to use the stage’s default), and source campaign.

The board (kanban)

  1. Each column is a stage, headed with its deal count, total value, win probability and weighted value. The bar above shows open deals, pipeline total and weighted forecast.
  2. Drag a card to another column to move it. The move is instant and saved in the background; if saving fails it snaps back with an error.
  3. While dragging, Won and Lost zones appear at the bottom of the screen. Drop on Won to win the deal, or on Lost to choose a loss reason.
  4. Filter the board by text or owner; switch pipelines with the pipeline selector.

Cards show value, expected close date (red when overdue), owner and a risk flag. Click a card’s title to open the deal.

List view

Switch Board / List (top right; your choice is remembered). The list adds weighted value and probability, inline stage changes, filters (status, stage, owner, close date), export and import.

The deal page

  • Stage path across the top: click any stage to move the deal there.
  • Won / Lost buttons (Lost asks for a reason: Price, Competitor, No budget, No decision, Timing, Missing features, Went silent, Other). Closed deals show Reopen.
  • Deal details: value, weighted value and probability, expected close, stage, contact, company, owner, dates, lost reason, custom fields.
  • Tabs: Timeline, Tasks, Quotes (chapter 9) and Stage history (who moved it, from where, when).

Deal risk flags

SignalTriggered when
Gone quietNo activity for 14+ days
StuckIn the same stage for 21+ days
OverdueExpected close date has passed
Low confidenceClosing within 7 days at under 50% probability
UnplannedNo expected close date set

One signal shows an amber Watch flag; two or more show a red At risk flag. Hover the flag to see the reasons.

Forecasting

Weighted value = deal value × win probability. Default stages: Qualification 10%, Discovery 25%, Proposal 50%, Negotiation 75%, Contract Sent 90%. Change stages and probabilities in Settings → Pipelines. The Forecast report groups open deals by expected close month into weighted, commit (75%+) and best case (50%+).

Chapter 9

Products and quotes

Keep a price list and send accurate quotes in minutes.

Product catalog Manager

Products & quotes → New product: name, SKU, unit price, description and Active. Inactive products stay on old quotes but can’t be added to new ones. The page also lists the most recent quotes across all deals.

Build a quote

  1. Open a deal → Quotes tab → New quote.
  2. For each line, pick a product (name and price fill in) or choose Custom item; set quantity, unit price and a line discount %. Add line for more.
  3. Set tax %, an overall discount amount, the valid-until date (default 30 days), status and notes/terms. Totals update as you type.
  4. Click Create quote. Quotes are numbered Q-0001, Q-0002… per organization.
TotalFormula
Line totalquantity × unit price − line discount %
Subtotalsum of line totals
Tax(subtotal − discount) × tax %
Totalsubtotal − discount + tax
  • Mark accepted sets the quote to Accepted and updates the deal’s value to the quote total.
  • PDF opens a clean printable quote; click Print / Save as PDF and choose “Save as PDF”.
  • Click a quote number to edit it.
Chapter 10

Activities and tasks

Record every interaction and never miss a follow-up.

Log an activity

  1. On any record click Log call, or ⋯ → Log meeting / Add note. Switch type with the Call / Meeting / Note buttons in the dialog.
  2. Enter a subject and details. For calls, choose an outcome (connected, left voicemail, no answer, wrong number, scheduled meeting) and a duration.
  3. To schedule a future call or meeting, fill in Scheduled for. It appears on the calendar and in the timeline with its time, and gets a Mark done button.

Logging a completed call, email or meeting automatically updates the contact’s last contacted date, the deal’s last activity (which clears “gone quiet” risk), and a lead’s first response and score.

Tasks

  • Create tasks from any record (Task button), from + New → Task, the Tasks page, a calendar day, or a dashboard action. Fields: title, due date, priority (low, medium, high, urgent), status (to do, in progress, done), assignee, Remind me at and notes.
  • Tasks page views: Overdue (red count badge), Today, Upcoming, All open and Completed. Filter by priority; managers can switch between My tasks and Team tasks.
  • Tick the box to complete a task; an Undo button appears in the confirmation. Click a task’s title to edit it.
  • Overdue due dates are shown in red. Reminders arrive in the notification bell at the time you set.
  • Assigning a task to someone else notifies them.

Calendar

  • Month, Week and Day views; arrows move backward and forward and Today returns to the current date.
  • Mine shows your own items; Team shows everything you’re allowed to see.

Chapter 13

Support tickets

Handle customer issues against a service-level deadline.

  • Create: + New → Ticket, the Tickets page, or New ticket on a contact. Fields: subject, contact, assignee, priority, status, channel (email, phone, chat, web) and description. Tickets are numbered #1, #2… per organization.
  • SLA deadline is set from priority. Changing priority recalculates it.
PriorityUrgentHighNormalLow
Resolve within4 hours8 hours24 hours72 hours
  • The list is sorted by SLA, with a live timer: grey normally, amber within 2 hours, red once breached. Resolved tickets show SLA met or Resolved late.
  • Statuses: Open → Pending → Resolved → Closed. Change status, priority and assignee from the ticket page or inline in the list.
  • The first logged reply (call, email, meeting) or status change records the first response time.
  • The Conversation tab holds every interaction; the Tasks tab holds follow-ups.
  • Assignees are notified of new tickets and of SLAs due within the hour or breached. Tickets also appear on the contact’s 360° page.
Chapter 14

Campaigns

Measure which marketing spend turns into revenue.

  1. New campaign Manager: name, channel (email, social, paid search, events, content, webinar, partner), status, start/end dates, budget and description.

Chapter 16

Automations

“When this happens, if these conditions are true, do that.” Let Nexora handle the busywork.

Build a workflow Manager

  1. Automations → New workflow, or start from a recipe.
  2. 1 · When pick a trigger.
  3. 2 · Only if (optional) add conditions on the record’s fields.
  4. 3 · Then add one or more actions, run in order.
  5. Save. Use the switch to pause or resume; Runs shows each run’s result (success, skipped, failed) and message.

Triggers

Deal is created · Deal moves to a stage · Deal is won · Deal is lost · Lead is created · Lead is converted · Contact is created · Ticket is created · Ticket status changes

Condition operators

Equals · Not equals · Greater than · Less than · Contains. Example: stage name equals Negotiation, value greater than 50000, score greater than 69.

Actions

Create task (title with merge tags, due in N days, priority, assign to owner / owner’s manager / a person) · Notify user · Assign owner (a person, or round-robin/territory rules) · Update field (deal probability, lead status, contact lifecycle/status, ticket priority/status) · Send email from a template.

Recipes

Follow up when a deal reaches Negotiation · Alert the manager about big new deals · Fast-track hot inbound leads · Escalate urgent tickets · Kick off onboarding on win.

Built-in notifications also fire without any workflow: owners are told when someone else moves or closes their deal, and when records or tasks are assigned to them.

Chapter 20

End-to-end walkthrough

From an empty workspace to a won deal, a happy customer and a report, in about fifteen minutes.

Set up (admin)

  1. Sign up. In Settings → Organization set your currency and turn on lead auto-assignment.
  2. In Settings → Pipelines, adjust stage names and win probabilities to match your sales process.
  3. In Settings → Users & quotas, add your reps with monthly quotas; create a team with a manager.
  4. Add a few products in Products & quotes, and an email template in Inbox → Templates.
  5. In Automations, enable the recipe “Follow up when a deal reaches Negotiation”.

Win a customer (rep)

  1. A prospect fills in your hosted form. The lead arrives scored, auto-assigned, and appears in the owner’s notifications and “What to do next”.
  2. Open the lead, click Log call and record the conversation. The first response time is captured and the lead becomes Contacted.
  3. Click Convert, keeping Also create a deal; set the value and expected close date.
  4. On the board, drag the deal through Discovery and Proposal. In the deal’s Quotes tab, build a quote from your products and click PDF to send it.
  5. Drag the deal to Negotiation: the automation creates a “Send contract” task for the owner.
  6. Use Email on the deal with your template to send the contract, then complete the task.
  7. Mark the quote accepted, then click Won. The contact becomes a Customer and revenue appears on the dashboard.

Support and review

  1. The customer reports an issue: create a ticket from their contact page. Work it before the SLA timer turns red, then resolve it.
  2. Managers switch to the Team dashboard to check quota coverage, then open Reports → Sales by rep and Pipeline health.
  3. Build a custom report (“sum of deal value by owner”), save it, and schedule it weekly to the leadership team.

Tips

  • Start each day on the dashboard’s What to do next list; it is ordered for you.
  • Set an expected close date on every deal; it removes a risk flag and powers the forecast.
  • Log every touchpoint; it keeps deals out of “gone quiet” and improves lead scores.
  • Save the filters you use daily as views, and share useful ones with your team.
  • Use Dark mode from the avatar menu for late sessions; the CRM works on phones and tablets too.

  • “You don’t have permission to do that”: your role lacks the permission (chapter 19); ask an admin.
  • A record has vanished: it may be owned by someone outside your view, or deleted. Admins can check the audit log.
  • “Can’t reach the server right now”: the server is restarting or offline; click Try again.
  • Can’t remove a stage or pipeline: move its deals to another stage first.
